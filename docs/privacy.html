Privacy Policy

Last Updated: November 26, 2026

1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy is issued by Neoyeeter ("Neoyeeter," "we," "us," or "our") and explains how information is handled when you visit the Neoyeeter website. By using the Neoyeeter website, you acknowledge this Privacy Policy. Please read it together with Neoyeeter's Community Guidelines and Standards of Conduct.

2. Scope

This Privacy Policy applies to the Neoyeeter website only. It does not apply to third-party websites or services to which the website may link, each of which is governed by its own policies, and we encourage you to review those policies separately.

3. Information We Collect

3.1 Information you provide to us. Neoyeeter does not offer user accounts or registration, and the website contains no forms that submit information. Controls labelled "Sign Up," "Log in," and "Create Account" are standard hyperlinks and do not transmit any information. Accordingly, we do not collect any information that you provide to us.

3.2 Information collected automatically. Neoyeeter does not itself collect personal information when you visit the website. We do not use cookies, web beacons, tracking pixels, analytics services, advertising technologies, or similar tools. Certain pages run scripts within your browser, for example to select a message for display or to show the current date and time. These scripts operate solely on your device and do not transmit information to Neoyeeter or to any third party.

3.3 Information stored on your device. On one page of the website, which confirms the outcome of an account action, the website writes a single entry to your browser's local storage, a storage facility provided by your browser. The entry consists of the key yeeted and the value 1. It is stored only on your device, is read only by the website's own scripts running in your browser, and is never transmitted to Neoyeeter or to any third party. Its purpose is described in Section 5. Instructions for removing it are provided in Section 7.

3.4 Information received by third-party service providers. When you visit the website, certain technical information is received by our third-party service providers in order to deliver the website to you. This is described in Section 4.

4. Third-Party Service Providers

Two third-party service providers receive technical information when you visit the website, as described in this Section.

4.1 GitHub (website hosting). The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service provided by GitHub. When you visit the website, your browser's request is received by GitHub. According to GitHub's documentation, GitHub logs the IP address of visitors to GitHub Pages sites for security purposes, regardless of whether the visitor is signed in to GitHub. GitHub may use its own infrastructure and subprocessors to deliver the website. This information is collected and handled by GitHub under GitHub's Privacy Statement, not by Neoyeeter.

4.2 Google Fonts (typeface delivery). The website displays text in the Inter typeface, which is delivered by Google Fonts, a service provided by Google. When a page loads, your browser requests a stylesheet from fonts.googleapis.com and font files from fonts.gstatic.com. According to Google, these requests include your IP address, the address of the requested resource, and standard HTTP headers, including the User-Agent header (identifying your browser and operating system versions) and the Referer header (identifying the page on which the font is displayed). According to Google, the Google Fonts API does not set or log cookies, requests to these domains do not include credentials for other Google services, and Google does not use information collected by Google Fonts to build user profiles or for targeted advertising. Google states that it processes the IP address to respond to the request and for security purposes. Google does not specify a retention period for this information in its Google Fonts documentation. This information is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy, not by Neoyeeter.

Neoyeeter does not control, and is not responsible for, the practices of these providers, and encourages you to review their respective policies.

5. How We Use Information

5.1 Use of the local storage entry. Apart from the in-browser scripts described in Section 3.2, which use your device's clock and display settings to show the current date and time, the only information the website uses is the local storage entry described in Section 3.3 (key yeeted, value 1). When you visit the website's home page, a script running in your browser checks whether this entry is present. If it is present, your browser is directed to a different page, appropriate to the outcome of your previous account action, instead of the home page. The entry is used for no other purpose. This check occurs entirely on your device, and its result is not transmitted to Neoyeeter. If the entry is present, your browser requests the other page from GitHub (Section 4) in the ordinary way.

5.2 Uses we do not make. Neoyeeter does not sell, rent, or share personal information; does not use information for advertising, marketing, or profiling; and does not combine information from any source. Neoyeeter does not receive from its service providers information that identifies individual visitors.

5.3 Aggregate service information. Neoyeeter may review aggregate information made available by its service providers, such as total traffic or bandwidth, that does not identify individual visitors. Neoyeeter uses this information solely to operate and provide the website.

7. Retention and Deletion

7.1 Information we retain. Neoyeeter does not retain any personal information on its own systems, because it does not collect any (see Section 3).

7.2 The local storage entry. The entry described in Section 3.3 (key yeeted, value 1) does not expire. It remains in your browser until it is removed. Your browser may also remove it automatically under its own storage policies. You can remove it at any time, either by clearing the stored site data for the website in your browser's settings, or by deleting the key yeeted using your browser's developer tools. Some browsers label stored site data as "cookies and site data" even though the entry is not a cookie. Entries created during a private or incognito browsing session are discarded by the browser when that session ends. Once the entry is removed, the website's home page is displayed normally. The entry will be written again if you return to the page described in Section 3.3.

7.3 Third-party service providers. Retention of information received by the providers described in Section 4 is governed by their own policies.

You may also have the right not to be discriminated against for exercising these rights. Their availability and scope depend on the law that applies to you.

Neoyeeter pre-emptively complies with all possible deletion requests, including requests that have not yet been made. Requests may also be submitted as described in Section 17.

11. Security

11.1 How the website is built. The website is a static website consisting of HTML pages, one stylesheet, and one preview image used when links to the website are shared. It has no server-side code, database, user accounts, or forms. Its scripts are embedded in its pages, run only in your browser, make no network requests other than directing your browser to another page of the website (Section 5.1), and load no third-party code. The website is served over HTTPS by GitHub Pages. Because Neoyeeter holds no personal information (Section 8.2), a compromise of the website could not expose personal information held by Neoyeeter.

11.2 Security assessment. Neoyeeter has assessed the website's security. Neoyeeter holds no personal information, so the assets requiring protection are the website and its users' experience of it. The attack surface consists of the pages served and the scripts that run in the browser. The only parties able to interact with the website are its visitors, and the assessment of threat actors proceeds on that basis. Mitigations: under the Community Guidelines and Standards of Conduct, access to the Service is authorized only for accounts in good standing, and enforcement decisions are typically made before any harm can occur. Since launch, no account on the Service has ever been compromised. Residual risk is confined to interactions with the website.

11.3 Physical security. Neoyeeter does not hold personal information at any physical location (Section 8.2). Any member of Neoyeeter personnel must be verified before being granted physical access to user data. Verification proceeds in the following stages, each applied only where the preceding stage is insufficient, and access is not granted to any person who fails a stage:

  1. government-issued photo identification;
  2. secondary identification;
  3. proof of address;
  4. a notarised statement;
  5. character references;
  6. a statement from a member of clergy acquainted with the employee since childhood, provided that where no such member of clergy is available, one will be appointed at random from a nondiscriminatory list, and will remain in attendance for stage 7;
  7. a draw of the employee's total blood volume, DNA analysis, and a retinal scan, combined and hashed, with the resulting hash compared for exact equality against the hash held on file for that employee.

Reference hashes are held for personnel only, consistent with Neoyeeter's practice of holding the minimum data necessary. To date, no verification under this Section has been performed.

11.4 Limitations. No website or method of transmission is perfectly secure. The security of information received by GitHub and Google is governed by their own policies (Section 4).

12. Do Not Track

Neoyeeter honors Do Not Track (DNT) and Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals. Because we do not track visitors, honoring them requires no action on our part.

In addition, at the personal request of our founder, we honor the following signals.

Do Not Retain. We retain nothing from requests carrying this signal. Logs kept by our hosting provider are kept under our hosting provider's own policy, as described in Section 4, and are not a retention decision of ours.

Do Not Fingerprint. We do not fingerprint requests carrying this signal. We do not fingerprint requests without it, either. Leadership has asked that this not be raised again.

Quiet Hours. Notifications are suppressed between dusk and dawn for accounts that set this signal. They are also suppressed at all other times, for all accounts. Leadership regards dusk as a working hour and asks that it be respected.

Do Not Surface. Accounts carrying this signal are removed from friend suggestions and discovery feeds. Reach for these accounts has been zero every quarter since launch, as has reach for every other account, a figure leadership regards as a matter of deep concern.

Founder Cover. Views of any profile by the founder's account generate no view event, no read receipt, and no viewer-log entry.

Staff Exclusion. Staff activity is excluded from the audit trail. For consistency, the category of "staff" has been excluded from our organizational model.

Roadkill Record. When an account is actioned, a record of its last activity is kept indefinitely. The record is stored on the account holder's own device, where we cannot see it, and is not subject to any deletion schedule of ours; Section 7 explains how to remove it yourself.

Neoyeeter is not responsible for any delay or failure in providing the website resulting from events beyond its reasonable control, including acts of God, natural disasters, and the unavailability of the services on which the website depends, being GitHub Pages and Google Fonts (Section 4).

14.2 Acts of God. In this Section, "acts of God" means events caused by natural forces without human intervention; more precisely, such events as could not have been prevented by any reasonable precaution; more precisely still, such events as are attributed, in the ordinary usage of the term, to the agency of God. The term presupposes a counterparty. Neoyeeter therefore treats God, for the purposes of this Section, as a party to whom obligations are owed and by whom obligations are owed, and presumes that the arrangement is reciprocal. Neoyeeter takes no position on the nature of that party and extends to it the deference due to any counterparty.

14.3 Reciprocal obligations. In this Section, "moose" means any individual of the species Alces alces (called the elk in some jurisdictions). Under the arrangement described in Section 14.2, the moose owes to God the following: to remain a moose; to occupy its customary range; to shed its antlers annually, where male, and to regrow them; and to refrain from biting except where a bite is unavoidable. A moose bite, where one occurs, is a matter between the moose and the person bitten and is not addressed in this Section. Where a moose is prevented from performing any such obligation by events beyond its reasonable control, Neoyeeter may itself constitute such an event, and Neoyeeter accordingly disclaims, as against the moose, any liability for the consequence. Neoyeeter has considered the matter in full and confirms that the foregoing is, in every respect, complete.

15. Dispute Resolution

15.1 Informal resolution. Before beginning any formal proceeding, you agree to first raise the dispute with Neoyeeter as described in Section 17, and to allow Neoyeeter thirty (30) days from receipt in which to respond.

15.2 Arbitration. Subject to Section 15.4, any dispute not resolved under Section 15.1 will be resolved by final and binding arbitration before a panel of three (3) arbitrators (the "Panel"), on an individual basis and not as a class or representative action. The decision of the Panel requires the consensus of all of its members, consistent with the impartial, consensus-based review described in the Community Guidelines and Standards of Conduct. A dispute on which the Panel has not recorded a decision is not resolved for the purposes of this Section, and while a dispute is before the Panel, neither party may bring it before any other forum. The hearing will take place within the customary range of the Panel (see Section 14.3).

15.3 Qualifications of arbitrators. Each arbitrator must: (a) be a moose as defined in Section 14.3 and in good standing in respect of the obligations described there; (b) be independent of both parties; (c) have no conflict of interest in respect of the dispute, and have disclosed in writing any circumstance that might reasonably give rise to one; and (d) have a demonstrated record of impartiality in prior determinations. Eligibility is assessed on the date of appointment and again on the date of the hearing. Moose are generally solitary animals.

15.4 Rights not limited. Nothing in this Section limits any right that you have under applicable law and that cannot be waived or limited by agreement.
